# Steady serve width and recoverable-time ledger, 2026-11-09

Read-only analysis of the 2026-11-09 three-agent steady runs. No GPU runs, builds,
or code changes. The numbers below are regenerated by
`docs/bench/analyze_serve_widths.py`; this report records the interpretation and
the provenance.

## Bottom line

- **B=3 is the workload.** It accounts for **82-88% of decode wall time** and
  **96.5-98.3% of observed member transitions** in the three default arms.
- **B=1 is effectively absent** (0-0.27% of wall, <0.04% of transitions). The only
  material low-width tail is **B=2**: 12-18% of wall but only 1.7-3.5% of tokens.
- Best-measured native decode rounds account for **65-85% of the 180 s window**.
  The remaining **15-35%** is append/prefill, scheduler/queue/transport, and
  visible multi-second stalls. GLM has the largest residual; Flash-Next the
  smallest.
- The largest lever is **reducing the B=3 (and B=4 at four agents) round cost**,
  not shifting the width distribution. The measured V4.1 12.3% B=3 cut delivered
  **+3.45%**; GLM's roughly 10-14% B=3/B=4 cuts delivered **+9.9% to +13.2%**;
  the FN equivalent is inferred at **5-8% per 10%**. All-at-B3 width coalescing
  is only **+0.5-0.7%**; even moving all B=1+B2 tokens to B=3 is **+2.5-4.0%**.
- **No B=4 occurs** in these three-agent windows. A fourth continuously active
  agent would improve native cost per token (B4 cost/4 is 12-16% below B3/3),
  but that is an extrapolation, not an observed width distribution.

## 1. Observed width distribution

arm is the later 180 s run. `wall` is exclusive open-stream overlap from the
trace (`decode_stream_width_*_s`), not native GPU batch service. `trans` is the
share of member-token transitions at that native width, derived from
`phases.batch` with the harness's own `native_widths` formula.

| GLM default | 180 s | 72.03 | 0.48 s (0.27%) | 28.47 s (15.82%) | 151.05 s (83.92%) | 0.00 s | 0.00 s | 0.00% | 2.83% | 97.17% |

Notes:

- The default arms have **zero cohort idle time and zero exclusive predecode
  time**: at least one stream is always decoding, and appends are interleaved
  with other streams. Per-agent tool gaps sum to 7.5 s V4.1, 3.8 s GLM, 1.7 s
  FN, but they are covered by peers and do not appear as cohort idle.
- The serial arms are the contrast: nearly all wall is B=1. GLM serial also
  spends 44.2 s with no decoder open (queue/prefill), which is why its
  `MAX_ACTIVE=1` rate is only 41.6 tok/s.
- The wall shares and transition shares differ because a B=3 round serves three
  tokens. B=2 consumes 12-18% of wall for only 1.7-3.5% of tokens; this is the
  expensive tail, not B=1.
- Later traces keep the same shape: V4.1 causal-on (90 s) is 19.4% B=2 / 80.6%
  B=3 wall; GLM B=4 causal-on (90 s) is 1.2% B=1 / 20.1% B=2 / 78.7% B=3.

## 2. Where the window goes

`S32`/`S64` are native decode service using the best measured whole-round costs
at 32K/64K and the observed width mix; `E = decode wall - S` is the residual.
The native costs include normal admission, full heads, and route/selection
validation. Append is the sum of per-request `prefill_ms` for turns sent inside
the window; these request-seconds overlap other decode and are not additive
wall. `>0.5 s gaps` sums visible trace `gap_max` values above 0.5 s.

| arm | delivered | S32 | S64 | E32 | E64 | append req-s | >0.5 s gaps |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| V4.1 default | 16,388 | 134.1 s (74.5%) | 143.6 s (79.8%) | 45.9 s (25.5%) | 36.4 s (20.2%) | 22.1 s | 11.9 s |
| GLM default | 12,966 | 117.2 s (65.1%) | 129.1 s (71.7%) | 62.8 s (34.9%) | 50.9 s (28.3%) | 20.6 s | 9.9 s |
| Flash-Next default | 17,091 | 153.9 s (85.5%) | 153.9 s (85.5%) | 26.1 s (14.5%) | 26.1 s (14.5%) | 15.1 s | 6.0 s |

Even after generously crediting every append request-second and every visible
>0.5 s gap as if it were on the critical path:

| arm | credited S + append + gaps (32K) | residual | credited at 64K | residual |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| V4.1 | 168.1 s | 11.9 s | 177.6 s | 2.4 s |
| GLM | 147.7 s | **32.3 s** | 159.6 s | **20.4 s** |
| Flash-Next | 175.0 s | 5.0 s | 175.0 s | 5.0 s |

The residual is the serving overhead not explained by native round costs. GLM's
is the largest: 11-18% of the window remains unaccounted even with that generous
credit. V4.1's residual is small at 64K but 12 s at 32K; FN's is about 5 s. Part
of the residual is the depth difference (actual roots are 48K+), and part is
queue/scheduler/transport work that these files do not break out.

## 3. Recoverable-at-N ledger

N=3 here. Counterfactuals keep all non-decode time fixed, except as noted. The
"native floor" removes all residual decode overhead and is an **absolute
ceiling**, not a credible single-step outcome. "All-at-B3" moves every member
transition to B=3; "B1+B2->B3" coalesces both low-width tails; "append 40%"
reduces append service by 40% with a 0.5 criticality credit.

| arm | current | native floor 32K | native floor 64K | all-at-B3 | B1->B3 | B1+B2->B3 | append 40% |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| V4.1 default | 91.04 | 122.19 (+34.2%) | 114.14 (+25.4%) | 91.65 (+0.7%) | 91.05 (+0.0%) | 94.72 (+4.0%) | 93.34 (+2.5%) |
| GLM default | 72.03 | 110.61 (+53.6%) | 100.44 (+39.4%) | 72.39 (+0.5%) | 72.03 (+0.0%) | 74.12 (+2.9%) | 73.72 (+2.3%) |
| Flash-Next default | 94.95 | 111.02 (+16.9%) | 111.02 (+16.9%) | 95.43 (+0.5%) | 94.96 (+0.0%) | 97.31 (+2.5%) | 96.57 (+1.7%) |

The native floor is large because S is only 65-85% of wall, but the measured
pairs show it is not all recoverable in one step. Realized pass-through from
the later work:

| lever | native change | delivered change | implied pass-through |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| V4.1 causal waves, B=3 32K | 28.157 -> 24.690 ms (-12.3%) | 94.344 -> 97.600 tok/s (**+3.45%**) | ~0.28 |
| GLM B=4 stack, B=4/B=3 32K | -13.7% / -11.8% | 68.289 -> 77.278 tok/s (**+13.16%**) | ~1.0 |
| GLM causal waves, B=4/B=3 32K | -9.7% / -9.4% | 78.356 -> 86.122 tok/s (**+9.91%**) | ~1.0 |
| GLM batched projections, B=3/B=4 | about -5.5% | 73.219 -> 76.839 tok/s (**+4.94%**) | ~0.9 |
| GLM small appends, 400 rows | 1088.91 -> 669.84 ms (-38.5%) | 72.92 -> 73.71 tok/s (**+1.07%**) | append is small |
| FN batching + direct outputs | serial -> default | 75.867 -> 94.950 tok/s (**+25.15%**) | width/ownership, not one kernel |
| FN append shapes | append pair | 88.361 -> 90.417 tok/s (**+2.33%**) | append is small |

So a practical reading of the ledger is:

- **V4.1:** the measured 12.3% B=3 cut gave **+3.45%** delivered, an implied
  pass-through of about 0.28; a linear 10% cut would be about 2.8%. This is far
  below the +34% native-floor ceiling. If the non-GPU stalls also shrink, the
  gain can be larger; the single 90 s pair cannot separate the two.
- **GLM:** a 10% B=3/B=4 cut is worth about **9-10%** delivered (measured). GLM
  is the most decode-service-bound of the three, and its later kernel work has
  the best pass-through.
- **FN:** a 10% B=3 cut is worth roughly **5-8%** delivered by inference. The
  batching stack already captured the huge serial-to-B3 step (+25.15%); the
  remaining B2/B3 post-stack costs are not published, so this is the least
  certain row.

## 4. Largest lever, and what is not explained

1. **B=3/B=4 round cost is the largest lever.** It is the only lever that is
   large in absolute terms and has measured pass-through. For V4.1/GLM the
   remaining work is at B=3 already; for FN the remaining B=3 cost is the next
   step after batching.
2. **Appends/prefill are second order.** They are 15-22 request-seconds but
   overlap other decode. A 40% append cut is worth only ~1.7-2.5% delivered in
   the ledger; measured GLM small-appends was +1.07% and FN append shapes
   +2.33%. Useful for TTFT/stalls, not for aggregate capacity.
3. **Width redistribution is not a meaningful lever here.** The B=1 tail is
   already gone. Moving every B=1+B2 token to B=3 is a generous +2.5-4.0%, and
   most of the B=2 wall is not native B=2 service; it is appends/stalls that
   would remain. "Fewer B=1 tails" is therefore the wrong target for this
   workload; the only expensive tail is B=2.
4. **Unexplained serving overhead is largest for GLM.** V4.1 has 36-46 s of
   residual decode overhead, GLM 51-63 s, FN ~26 s. After crediting appends and
   visible long gaps, GLM still has 20-32 s (11-18% of window) unexplained;
   V4.1 has 2-12 s; FN ~5 s. That ordering is consistent with GLM's larger
   measured gains from B=4 scheduling/kernel changes and with V4.1's lower
   pass-through (native savings partly absorbed by non-GPU stalls). More
   append/host tracing would be needed to assign the GLM residual.

## 5. Uncertainty and thin data

- **Transition coverage is incomplete.** Cutoff-partial calls have no native
  usage, so their width counters are missing: V4.1 7,490/16,388 delivered
  tokens (46%), GLM 3,388/12,966 (26%), FN 528/17,091 (3%). The transition
  shares apply the covered-call mix to those tokens. The wall-share trace covers
  all calls, so it is the more complete width observation. If the partial calls
  had a different width mix, the transition shares would move.
- **Depth.** Native costs are measured at 32K and (mostly) 64K, while the
  window starts at 48K and grows. The ledger reports both bounds; the true S is
  between them. V4.1/GLM B=2 at 64K was not measured; it uses the 32K value.
- **FN B2/B3 are the weakest inputs.** The published B=1..4 table predates the
  later batching stack. Only B4 has a final production standing (30.12 ms at
  32K). B3=27.0 ms here is inferred from throughput/native B4, with a plausible
  range of about 24.2-30.1 ms; the FN ledger and residual should be read as
  provisional.
- **Single-window pairs.** The later V4.1/GLM/FN pairs are one or two windows,
  not ABBA; natural EOS and unequal work are normal. The native ABBA packets are
  stronger evidence for round costs; the delivered-rate pairs are descriptive.
- **Two different width notions.** Trace width is open-stream overlap, not
  native GPU batch width. Transition width comes from the engine's batch
  counters. The report keeps them separate and does not treat stream overlap as
  GPU service time.
- **No B=4 observation.** The three-agent windows never reach B=4. The N=4
  statement is an extrapolation from native B4 costs, not a measured width
  distribution.
- **Engine versions differ.** The 180 s distribution is engine `9b193b38`;
  the later pairs are `fe0df5796`/`a9310ecc` (V4.1), `45687e25`/`ce581ae50`
  (GLM), and `eaf39c4f` (FN). Using the baseline distribution with the best
  later costs is deliberate for the "best measured" ledger, but it is not a
  same-engine A/B.
